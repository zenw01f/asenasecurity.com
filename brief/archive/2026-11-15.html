Lead storyHighZero-day · Firewall · Wiper

Check Point ships emergency fix for a root-access flaw already exploited in the wild, and one intrusion ended with a wiper on an ESXi host

CVE-2026-123039 was being used against a couple dozen customers as far back as late October, according to Check Point's advisory.

Check Point released hotfixes for a local privilege-escalation bug in the Gaia OS component behind its dynamic CLI. A daemon running as root listens on a world-writable local socket and does not check who is talking to it, so any authenticated local user can get commands run as root. BleepingComputer reports that Check Point's advisory (SK1000362) says exploitation began in late October and has hit "a couple of dozen customers." Affected products include Firewall, Security Gateway, Security Management Server, Multi-Domain Security Management and Scalable Platforms.

A separate Check Point report, summarized by BleepingComputer and The Hacker News, describes how attackers got in through PHP2Shell vulnerabilities in the Mobile Access portal, used this flaw to reach root, planted web shells and backdoors (TSGA and TSProxy), then moved to a VMware ESXi host and deployed a wiper. For teams that cannot patch right away, the stopgap is to stop the dynamic_server process and keep it from restarting. The coverage read for this edition does not name a threat actor.

Why it matters: Security appliances sit at the edge with broad trust, and a bug that needs only a low-privilege foothold turns any earlier portal flaw into full control. Patch, then look for web shells and unexpected admin accounts, and make sure hypervisors are not reachable from firewall management networks. The wiper step is a reminder to test offline backups of virtualization hosts.

HighAI-enabled attacks · FBI

FBI flash alert: open-source AI pentest framework ARTEX is being used in real intrusions, some reaching privileged access in under three hours

The FBI says multiple threat actors have used ARTEX, a Go-based tool that drives large language models through reconnaissance, exploitation, privilege escalation and data theft with little human input. BleepingComputer reports activity since at least August and more than 1,600 publicly exposed ARTEX consoles as of early November. Attacks linked to it include a ConnectWise cloud-backup supply-chain breach and intrusions at South Korean banks.

The original code was pulled from GitHub, but forks and a paid edition are still circulating, so takedowns are unlikely to slow it. The advisory includes indicators of compromise and asks victims to report to IC3. The Record adds that Amazon and Microsoft published related research the same week.

Why it matters: Assume attackers can now move from first access to privileged access in hours, not days. Shorten detection and response targets, tighten egress and credential hygiene, and review how quickly alerts on new admin activity are triaged.

How the sources differ
  • BleepingComputer says the original repository appeared in July and was removed October 8; The Record says the tool was released on GitHub in August.

HighWindows · Public exploit

Public SignDrop exploit claims SYSTEM access on Windows through the CLFS driver; Microsoft has not confirmed a flaw or issued a fix

A researcher publishing as MSNightmare posted proof-of-concept code on GitHub on the same day as Microsoft's November updates. The Hacker News reports that it abuses a log-authentication feature added in October 2025 updates and claims a standard user can reach SYSTEM; it was tested on Windows 11 26H2 and Windows Server 2025 builds.

One researcher who checked it said the exploit works but is not reliable enough for operational use. Per The Hacker News there is no CVE, no patch, no KEV listing and no reported attacks yet, though the same researcher's earlier Defender exploits were later patched. Exploit code that is public and untested tends to be adapted quickly.

Why it matters: Local privilege escalation is what turns a phished user into a domain problem. Confirm endpoint detection covers privilege-escalation behavior, limit local admin, and watch for Microsoft guidance in the coming days.

HighSupply chain · Credentials

Campaign used 4,837 hijacked GitHub accounts to open pull requests that steal cloud and CI secrets

BleepingComputer reports a campaign dubbed wfx-loot that probed more than 61,000 repositories from compromised accounts beginning November 10. It opened pull requests titled as dependency-audit notes and delivered a roughly 1,500-line script that harvests GitHub Actions secrets, cloud credentials and AI-service keys. Researchers confirmed theft from at least 1,835 targets.

The payload also reached a Packagist package and a Go module through automated publishing. Aikido, which reported the activity, found most of the malicious pull requests still open. Owners are advised to look for a wfx-docs branch and rotate any secrets and cloud roles that ran the payload.

Why it matters: Treat CI/CD secrets as production credentials. Scope them narrowly, require review before workflows from outside contributors run, prefer short-lived cloud tokens, and have a rotation runbook ready.

HighBreach · AI tooling

A leaked vendor key led to a Cursor data breach and a trojanized Claude Code extension in its marketplace

The Hacker News reports that Cursor said an access key it issued to Fireworks AI was exposed on Fireworks' side. An attacker used it to copy old coding-agent traces from a read-only storage bucket, then used credentials found inside those traces to reach internal SaaS tools. A trojanized build of the Claude Code extension was served through the Cursor marketplace for about 11 hours on October 29, with up to 5,000 users potentially affected.

Uber separately said a service provider's credentials were used to access its data, and TechCrunch reported that provider as Fireworks AI, per The Hacker News. The pattern is a third party's leaked key reaching a customer's data, then secrets hiding in logs and traces extending the damage.

Why it matters: Agent logs and traces often contain live credentials. Scrub them, scope vendor-issued keys tightly, and add AI vendors and developer-tool marketplaces to third-party risk reviews.

MediumExtortion · Logistics

Maersk confirms data theft from an inherited IT system as WhiteArmor claims a $5 million ransom demand

SecurityWeek reports that Maersk said attackers took data from one IT system it gained through a 2022 acquisition, that the incident is isolated, and that its shipping platforms are unaffected. The WhiteArmor group listed Maersk on its leak site on November 7 and claims about 100GB of data.

Maersk has not confirmed WhiteArmor's involvement or what data was taken, and the group's claims are unverified. The piece underlines how acquired systems can carry risk long after a deal closes.

Why it matters: Inventory and segment systems inherited through acquisitions, and add them to patching, logging and backup coverage early. Extortion claims should be treated as unverified until the victim confirms.

OutageResilience · Cloud

Multi-hour Microsoft 365 outage hits Outlook, Teams and Copilot across several countries; Microsoft says it sees no malicious activity

SecurityWeek, carrying an AP report, says the disruption ran from Thursday into Friday and affected Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams and Copilot, with government bodies and businesses in the UK, Australia, Japan and elsewhere reporting problems. Microsoft said it found no sign of an attack and was restoring service in phases.

Australia's home affairs minister reportedly compared it to the 2024 CrowdStrike update fault. Separately, the FAA briefly halted departures nationwide on Friday; officials found no sign of hacking and the FBI is reportedly looking at a software update (see Quick Hits).

Why it matters: Single-vendor dependence is a risk even without an attacker. Confirm clients have an out-of-band way to communicate during an email or collaboration outage, and that incident plans do not rely on the same tenant.

Cyber consolidation: CrowdStrike and Palo Alto are buying or courting AI-agent security startups at about $500M apiece

This item sits where cyber meets markets, and the reporting predates this edition's window but sets context for the radar below. TechCrunch reported on November 3 that CrowdStrike will pay nearly $500 million for two AI security startups, and on November 5 that Palo Alto Networks is in talks to buy agent-security startup Clutch for $500 million. Only headlines were available, so terms beyond those figures are not reported here.

Talks are not a signed deal. Large platform vendors are paying up for agent-security capabilities while venture money flows into the same niche; this week's rounds for Daylight and WhiteMatter are in the radar section.

Why it matters: Vendor consolidation can change roadmaps, pricing and support for tools clients already run. Ask key vendors how acquisitions affect their product commitments.
