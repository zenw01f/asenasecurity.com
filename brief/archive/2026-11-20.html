Lead storyCriticalZero-Day · VMware · Chinese APT · vCenter

China-linked Armory Castle exploited VMware vCenter as a zero-day for months before Broadcom knew; CVSS-9.8 auth bypass creates full admin session on every vSphere version

An unauthenticated flaw in VMware Directory Service let hackers silently become vsphere.local administrators, then steal SAML signing keys, forge tokens, and exfiltrate VM disk contents — Broadcom released patches on November 19 after Google disclosed an intrusion from September.

CVE-2026-123890 (CVSS 9.8) is an unauthenticated session-disclosure bug in VMware Directory Service's implementation of the Secure Remote Password (SRP-6a) protocol over DCE/RPC. By manipulating the SRP handshake an attacker on the network — no credentials required — can recover the session key and open an authenticated session as administrator@vsphere.local, the highest-privilege built-in account in a vSphere environment. All major vCenter versions are affected: 7.0 (now out of general support), 8.0, Cloud Foundation 5.2.x, and vSphere Foundation 9.0.x and 9.1.x. Broadcom released patches November 19 — vCenter 8.0 U3m, Cloud Foundation 5.2.5, vSphere Foundation 9.0.2.0200 and 9.1.1.0200 — but vCenter 7.0 customers must hold an active extended-support contract to receive a fix.

The exploiting group, Armory Castle (also tracked as UNC5221 and Warp Panda, assessed by Google as Chinese espionage), was already inside a target environment via a separate BeyondTrust zero-day (CVE-2026-102764) when it pivoted to vCenter in early September 2026 using a custom Golang exploitation utility named BONESAW. After gaining administrator@vsphere.local access the attackers moved with unusual thoroughness: they exfiltrated the LMDB configuration database, hijacked the SAML Security Token Service, stole the STS signing certificate, created new SSO administrator accounts, forged SAML tokens to impersonate users across connected systems, mounted VMFS volumes to access VM disk images directly, and obtained the vCenter master encryption key and database credentials. Five malware families — SLIMSAVAGE, CAGEMATCH, SAWSNAKE, BEARBONES, and LAZYBONES — were deployed for persistence and continued exfiltration. Google's Threat Intelligence Group published the full analysis on November 19, coinciding with Broadcom's patch release.

The two-and-a-half-month gap between exploitation and disclosure means organizations should approach this as a potential active-compromise scenario rather than a standard patching exercise. CISA had not yet added CVE-2026-123890 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog as of publication, but active exploitation is confirmed. After patching, administrators should audit vsphere.local for unexpected administrator accounts, review SAML token issuance logs for anomalous user impersonation, and inspect every ESXi host's SSH authorized_keys files for unauthorized additions. The SAML signing-key theft capability is particularly dangerous because forged tokens can reach Azure Active Directory, any identity provider accepting SAML assertions, and downstream SaaS applications — the blast radius extends well beyond the vCenter console.

Why it matters: VMware vCenter is the administrative crown jewel of enterprise data centers — control vCenter, control every VM and ESXi host it manages. The SAML token-forgery capability means a successful attacker can impersonate any user across every system that trusts the vCenter identity provider, including cloud and SaaS platforms. For clients running vCenter, verify patch status today and treat any inability to confirm a clean bill of health as grounds for incident-response engagement rather than waiting. Organizations on vCenter 7.0 without an extended-support contract should firewall the vCenter management network immediately while they arrange access to the patch.

HighBreach · MSP · Email Security

Attackers breach N-able's email security cloud infrastructure; Mail Assure and SpamExperts management portals go dark across all three global regions

Unknown threat actors gained unauthorized access to a server inside N-able's Mail Assure and SpamExperts Hosted Cloud on November 16; N-able confirmed the breach November 19. The intrusion disabled management portals, APIs, quarantine functions, and audit logging for Americas, APAC, and Europe deployments simultaneously, though core mail-flow filtering continued operating. N-able stated it has "no evidence that customer data was compromised, including access to tenants' mailboxes" and has since taken hardening steps and disabled affected interfaces.

This is a separate incident from the N-central 17 zero-day chain covered in Edition #003; it involves N-able's cloud-hosted email filtering product, not on-premises RMM infrastructure. Because MSPs use Mail Assure and SpamExperts to filter email for multiple downstream client organizations, management-layer access could expose email quarantine contents and routing metadata for every organization they serve — the actual end-customer population is significantly larger than the direct N-able partner count. The investigation was ongoing as of November 20, and "no evidence of compromise" in an in-progress investigation is not conclusive.

Why it matters: MSPs handling email security on behalf of clients should audit all N-able Mail Assure and SpamExperts tenant access logs from November 16 onward, verify that no email-forwarding rules or transport rules were modified, and proactively notify clients that an investigation is in progress. Two N-able incidents in one week also signals that the platform is under sustained targeting — a pattern worth flagging at the board level for clients whose MSP uses N-able products.

CriticalPatch · RCE · PHP · Web Infrastructure

PHP ships emergency fixes for seven critical flaws including phpAfterShell session-hijack-to-RCE and three separate CVSS-9.2 vulnerabilities affecting every supported PHP branch

The PHP Foundation released PHP 8.2.36, 8.3.37, 8.4.28, 8.5.13, and 8.6.0 on November 19, patching seven critical vulnerabilities. CVE-2026-126714 (CVSS 9.2), dubbed phpAfterShell, is a time-of-check/time-of-use race in file-based session handling: an attacker who interrupts a session write can inject malicious serialized objects that PHP deserializes when the session resumes, enabling code execution via PHP gadget chains. CVE-2026-115560 (CVSS 9.2) is a broad input-validation flaw enabling RCE, file modification, and session corruption. CVE-2026-129289 (CVSS 9.2) affects any PHP deployment behind a persistent FastCGI proxy — nginx, Apache mod_proxy_fcgi, HAProxy, or LSAPI — and exploits inconsistent HTTP request interpretation. Four additional use-after-free bugs round out the patch set.

phpAfterShell depends on file-based session storage, which is the PHP default for applications that have not explicitly configured Redis or Memcached. Timing the race condition requires effort, but public proof-of-concept tools are already circulating. PHP powers the vast majority of server-side web applications; the affected versions (8.2–8.6) are all actively supported branches. Organizations running PHP in Docker containers should rebuild images; those relying on OS-packaged PHP should check distro security feeds for updated packages.

Why it matters: PHP is the substrate for a large share of custom web applications, e-commerce platforms, and CMS deployments. Any client with a PHP-based web presence should treat this as a priority patch this sprint cycle. The phpAfterShell variant is especially relevant for legacy codebases using file-based sessions to store sensitive application state.

HighEnd-of-Life · Edge Device · Firewall

SonicWall declares all Generation 6 firewalls will receive no further security patches and urges immediate retirement as AI-accelerated exploitation shrinks the window for edge devices

SonicWall announced that its Generation 6 product line — TZ300 through TZ600, NSA 2650 through NSA 6600, SOHO series, SuperMassive 9200 through 9800, NSv 10 through NSv 1600 virtual appliances, and NSsp models running SonicOS 6.x — will receive no further security patches under any circumstances, including for critical vulnerabilities. The company cited AI-assisted exploitation of edge devices, stating "attackers now use automation and AI to find and exploit unsupported edge devices faster than ever." Google Mandiant research was specifically referenced showing that mean time to exploit has gone negative: exploitation now begins, on average, seven days before patches become available for newly disclosed vulnerabilities.

SonicWall recommends immediate retirement and migration to Generation 8 hardware running SonicOS 8. For organizations that cannot immediately replace devices, the company recommends disabling WAN management access and enforcing multi-factor authentication on administrative interfaces — characterizing these as delay tactics, not mitigations. Gen 6 firewalls are among the most common edge appliances in SMB and distributed-enterprise environments; organizations conducting inventory are likely to find forgotten units on network segments outside their primary refresh cycle.

Why it matters: Gen 6 SonicWall firewalls are common in exactly the client base a vCISO often serves. Any client still running them is now operating a permanently unpatched edge appliance that sophisticated automated scanners will increasingly target. Add Gen 6 SonicWall inventory to your next client risk review, prioritize those facing the internet directly, and use this announcement as leverage in budget conversations about hardware refresh cycles.

HighGovernment · AI-Assisted Attack · Attribution Pending

European Commission confirms October cyberattack that disrupted HR, medical, and translation services for more than ten days; attackers used an AI-powered penetration tool

The European Commission disclosed November 19–20 that attackers breached its environment in an incident detected October 22 and confirmed October 26 — with disruptions to HR platform Sysper, the MyPMO medical reimbursement system, and translation services persisting for more than ten days while affected applications were taken offline. Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier stated the investigation "has found no evidence of a data breach affecting the Commission's staff," though Politico separately reported that large amounts of data may have been accessed. No threat group claimed responsibility and no ransom demand was received.

Regnier confirmed attackers used an AI tool during the breach, without specifying which one. The disclosure follows a CERT-EU warning about Artex, an AI-powered penetration-testing framework, which the agency says "will likely accelerate adoption by threat actors, resulting in more frequent, faster, and more scalable automated attacks with a higher success rate." The FBI independently warned in a separate November alert that Artex enables privileged access in under three hours using legitimate credentials and overlooked service accounts — a technique consistent with what the Commission described. The case illustrates a capability shift: AI is being used not only to accelerate phishing but to navigate internal environments once a foothold is established.

Why it matters: For clients with internal service portals, HR platforms, or benefits and medical reimbursement systems, the question is whether an attacker with any foothold could use an AI-assisted tool to escalate faster than defenders can detect. CERT-EU's recommended signature to monitor: fast, low-noise lateral movement through legitimate credentials on neglected legacy systems — particularly internal portals built by individual business units outside the core IT estate.

HighBreach · Healthcare · Japan · SEC Filing

SBC Medical Group discloses two data breaches affecting more than 2 million patients and employees in Japan, including 20,000 employee records with usernames and passwords

Japanese cosmetic clinic operator SBC Medical Group filed a material disclosure on November 20 reporting two separate intrusions collectively affecting over 2 million individuals. The primary breach exposed personal data for 1.6 million patients — names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, dates of birth, gender, patient card numbers, and survey responses — along with treatment details and image file names for smaller subsets. Twenty thousand employee records were also accessed, including employee IDs and passwords. Fourteen credit card numbers were in the exposed data, though all were expired or inactive. A secondary breach through the company's Tribeau reservation service affected an additional 19,000 customers.

SBC Medical Group, publicly traded in Japan, stated it is still assessing the financial impact. Beyond the patient-record exposure, 459 employees had bank account information, pension details, and tax certificates accessed — data enabling identity fraud well beyond phishing. The employee password exposure is a lateral-movement risk: credential reuse from HR or operational systems into corporate applications or identity providers is a common follow-on attack path. SBC has not disclosed the attack vector, timeline of compromise, or identity of the threat actor.

Why it matters: This case illustrates a persistent gap in healthcare security: operational data (patient records) and HR data (employee credentials, financial details) often share infrastructure or access controls despite having very different sensitivity profiles. For clients in healthcare or life sciences, verify that HR and patient systems have distinct access boundaries and that employee credential stores are excluded from application database backups that might be accessible to a less-privileged attacker.

Equities drop sharply Thursday as VIX spikes to 30.49, erasing the week's gains across all major indices in a broad risk-off session

Yahoo Finance's Thursday November 19 close data shows a broad sell-off: the S&P 500 fell 2.02% to 7,175.83, the Nasdaq dropped 2.25% to 23,879.20, and the Dow shed 2.19% to 49,097.06. Gold declining alongside equities — rather than rising as a traditional safe haven — suggests potential forced selling or broad portfolio deleveraging rather than pure flight to safety. With the VIX above 30 and 10-year yields sustained above 5%, private cybersecurity-vendor valuations and late-stage financing conditions remain under meaningful pressure. These conditions also affect year-end M&A and IPO pricing windows for the sector.

BreachFinance · Third-party · Extortion · Cyber × Markets

N0n extortion group leaks data tied to JPMorgan, Visa, and Mastercard marketing vendors; all three say their own systems were not breached and no payment data was exposed

The N0n extortion group, which has claimed roughly 200 victims since emerging in September 2026, published data it attributed to JPMorgan Chase, Visa, and Mastercard on November 16. All three financial firms confirmed the exposure was real but attributed it to third-party marketing vendors rather than their own systems. JPMorgan reported that attackers accessed "marketing data held by those vendors," including over 16,000 customer email addresses — some with phone numbers and postal codes — plus about 124 internal employee email addresses. Visa confirmed a third-party provider in Asia Pacific was involved, with customer emails, phone numbers, and marketing campaign records exposed. Mastercard described "a limited amount of email addresses — non-payments information — from a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program." None of the three companies reported payment card data, account credentials, or financial account information in the exposed datasets.

The pattern — large-brand exposure via marketing technology or analytics vendor — reflects a structural vulnerability in how financial services firms outsource customer engagement. Marketing data is typically treated as lower-sensitivity than payment data, leading to softer vendor security requirements and less rigorous monitoring of outbound data flows. High-net-worth cardholder email addresses now in threat-actor hands creates a targeted phishing risk particularly acute for JPMorgan's premium Reserve card program customers. None of the three companies have been marked as having paid on the N0n group's leak site. This story sits at the cyber-meets-markets intersection: it is filed here rather than in the Cybersecurity section because the primary business implication is third-party vendor risk management at systemically important financial institutions.

Why it matters: For clients in financial services, review marketing technology vendor contracts: who has CRM or email list access, what security requirements govern that access, and whether "marketing data" is explicitly scoped out of more rigorous vendor risk assessments. Data classified as "not payment-related" can still be highly sensitive when it identifies specific high-value customers by product or benefit program.
